\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Geography in language models.}
\citet{gurnee2024space} fit linear probes from the hidden states of base language models to the coordinates of places and report high $R^2$ for latitude and longitude. Their headline measure does not separate position within a country from knowing the country, and they do not study cosine similarity. We show that, when the text names the country, a country centroid alone reaches $R^2$ of 0.95/0.98, so we measure gain over that centroid instead. \citet{decoupes2025distortions} evaluate geographical distortions in ten language models, including the correspondence between cosine and geographic distances, and report uneven representation across regions. \citet{desabbata2025geospatial} apply spatial analysis to probe and sparse-autoencoder features of place names. These works analyse representations. They neither compare base and retrieval models nor try to change what similarity reflects.

\paragraph{Adding or recovering geography.}
Several methods add geographic knowledge through training. GeoLM incorporates coordinates and geographic context into language representations \citep{li2023geolm}. \citet{srinivasagan2026location} pair place names with latitude and longitude during training. These methods need coordinates or fine-tuning, while our remedy keeps the model frozen and uses no coordinates at query or index time. Closest to our work, \citet{feuerstein2026recovering} recover geographic and temporal structure from the output embeddings of commercial models by projecting onto axes built from a few seed pairs. They report global rank correlations with latitude and longitude, not within-country neighbour quality, and they do not compare base with retrieval models. Their projection is the natural baseline for our remedy; we describe a validation comparison in Section~\ref{sec:method-baselines}. In a literature scan in November 2026 we found no work that compares base and retrieval models on within-country cosine neighbours, or that proposes a coordinate-free fix for it. The scan was not exhaustive.

\paragraph{Probing methodology.}
A probe shows that information is decodable, not that a model uses it. \citet{hewitt2019control} argue that probe accuracy must be read against control tasks; our random-initialisation and country-centroid controls follow this advice. Structural probes learn a low-rank linear map whose squared distances match a target distance \citep{hewitt2019structural}. Our distance probe adapts this idea to geographic neighbour ranking within countries, with a triplet loss instead of a regression.

\paragraph{Layers of embedding models.}
Intermediate layers of language models often give better representations than the final layer \citep{skean2025layer}. \citet{jiang2026layerwise} compare embedding models with their base language models layer by layer, without a geographic task. Our depth profile adds a geographic, similarity-based view, and our layer fusion exploits it.

\paragraph{Text embedding models and instructions.}
Recent embedding models are built from decoder language models and trained with contrastive retrieval objectives: e5-mistral from Mistral-7B \citep{wang2024improving,jiang2023mistral}, Qwen3-Embedding from Qwen3 \citep{zhang2025qwen,qwen3technicalreport}, and bge-multilingual-gemma2 from Gemma-2 \citep{gemmateam2024gemma2} in the BGE family \citep{xiao2023cpack}. Encoder-based models such as multilingual E5 \citep{wang2024e5} and BGE-M3 \citep{chen2024m3} build on XLM-R \citep{conneau2019xlmr}. Many of these models take a task instruction with the input \citep{su2023instructor,wang2024improving}. We use the instruction as a handle that changes which facet of a text the similarity tracks. Embeddings are often anisotropic, which can hide structure from raw cosine similarity; removing the mean and top principal components is a standard correction \citep{mu2018allbutthetop}, and we use it as a control.
