\begin{abstract}
A query such as ``nearest rock station'' asks a text-embedding search system for geographic proximity, but the system ranks candidates by cosine similarity. We ask two questions. Does a language model know where a place lies \emph{within} its country? And does the cosine similarity used for search reflect that knowledge? We probe every layer of 19 checkpoints, including base models paired with retrieval models trained from them, on 34,196 cities. We measure probes against a country-centroid control and reflected geography with a within-country nearest-neighbour score. On held-out test cities, every decoder from 4B parameters up beats the country centroid by 116--375~km, and retrieval training keeps this signal decodable. Cosine similarity reflects it only in part. Output vectors of most base models show little of it, and the best retrieval model reaches about a third of the true-distance ceiling (0.25--0.27 of 0.73--0.75). A cheap remedy needs no coordinates at query or index time and is fit on training cities only. It combines a geography task instruction, fusion with an intermediate layer, and a rank-32 distance probe. For two retrieval models it raises within-country neighbour quality on test cities from 0.20--0.26 to 0.35--0.38, about half the ceiling, and STS-Benchmark correlation does not drop relative to the uninstructed model. On 32,148 fresh held-out towns, tested once, the remedy still helps in every run. Its gain comes from the geographic wording, not from using an instruction, and seed-pair coordinate axes do not reproduce it. Applied unchanged to five retrieval models from other teams, it raises within-country neighbour quality for every model above 0.6B parameters, in all 18 runs for three of them, down to towns of 1,000 inhabitants. Its gain grows with model size within two model families, and at 0.6B it vanishes beyond large cities. In a station-search task with genre queries near a town, it beats each model's standard web-search use on average in every run once mislocated stations are removed, but clears our strict all-runs interval test only for harrier-27B and e5-mistral. We froze every claim and its pass criterion before its single test run. Among the earlier claims, three narrowly failed their own criteria, and we report them: that masked-language encoders up to 3.5B show no within-country geography (40 of 42 runs), that every retrieval output vector beats the country centroid (11 of 12), and that retrieval output vectors beat their bases in all 48 cosine comparisons (46 of 48).
\end{abstract}
