\head{Progress since the proposal}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Probing pipeline (complete).} Ridge probes on every layer's pooled hidden state for 34,196 GeoNames cities and 11,833 radio stations; 10\textdegree{} spatial-block and random splits, 3 seeds, block-bootstrap CIs, a country-centroid control, untouched test split.
\item \textbf{Models.} 18 checkpoints: five decoder \emph{base$\to$retrieval} pairs (Qwen3 0.6B/4B/8B; Mistral-7B$\to$e5-mistral; Gemma-2-9B$\to$bge-multilingual-gemma2), XLM-R base/large with E5 and the BGE-M3 stages, two random-init controls.
\item \textbf{Validation results (all 18 models; test not yet run).} A \emph{country-residual} probe (a place's offset from its country's centroid) finds within-country geography in every decoder from 4B up: at the best layer it beats the country centroid by 12\,/\,24, 120\,/\,152 and 175\,/\,201\,km for Qwen3 0.6B\,/\,4B\,/\,8B, 248\,/\,304 for Mistral-7B and 303\,/\,285 for Gemma-2-9B (base\,/\,retrieval; cities); encoders stay near 0. Base and retrieval CIs overlap in all five pairs: retrieval training keeps this information. Absolute-location probes on the Qwen3-8B, e5-mistral and bge-multilingual-gemma2 retrieval models gain 311--459\,km on cities, none on stations.
\item \textbf{Retrieval.} A frozen run (5 models, 11,479 stations, 45 genres, 12 origins): changing the query origin replaces 8--21\% of the top 10, yet regret against the true nearest stays at 3,668--4,141\,km; adding the origin changes regret by $-170$\,km (CLAP) to $+385$\,km (EmbeddingGemma).
\item \textbf{Visualization.} \emph{Built:} the globe explorer (origin/genre selection, true vs.\ model top-$k$ on a 3-D globe, audio), the basis of V1; static depth curves with CIs for all 18 models; a static true$\to$predicted arrow map (Qwen3-8B). \emph{Planned:} interactive V2, V3, linking.
\end{itemize}

\head{Response to feedback}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Visualization contribution.} Three views share one selection (model pair, layer, region, place), so a pattern seen in one is checked in the others.
\emph{(V1) Globe ranking:} the model's top 10 and the true 10 nearest as arcs from the origin, colored same-country\,/\,same-language\,/\,other, so border stops and language jumps show; a panel gives the query's metrics against the random-ordering band.
\emph{(V2) Layer-by-region map:} 10\textdegree{} blocks colored by probe gain over the country centroid. Brushing a layer range on the depth curve updates the map; toggles switch model, probe target and base$-$retrieval difference; clicking a block shows its depth curve with CI, its places in V3, and sets V1's origin there. \emph{Signal vs.\ noise:} a block is colored only with $\ge$30 places and a CI excluding 0 after multiple-comparison correction (else hatched); the random-init model gives a tokenization-only reference map.
\emph{(V3) Distortion map:} each block shows its mean displacement (predicted minus true, local km) with a bootstrap CI. To separate overlapping pulls, displacements are regressed jointly on unit vectors toward the capital, the country centroid and the continent centroid; blocks where these directions nearly coincide are marked not identifiable. Difference arrows show what retrieval training moves.
\emph{Evaluation (no IRB):} case studies C1--C4 below, each confirmed by a number, and a think-aloud walkthrough with 2--3 NLP/GIS researchers.
\item \textbf{Critique of key papers.} \emph{Decoupes et al.} use final embeddings of isolated city names only, so geography never learned cannot be told apart from geography lost before the output; they measure distance correspondence, not whether distortions change search results. \emph{Gurnee \& Tegmark} probe base-LM layers with $R^2$ on raw latitude/longitude, which rewards knowing the country: on our data the country centroid alone gives $R^2=0.95$ (latitude), and a ridge probe on a frozen, randomly initialized Qwen3-0.6B reaches 0.78 when the text names the country. Probes show decodability, not use, and they cover generation models only. \emph{Our fixes:} layer-wise probes, a country-residual target with both controls, retrieval metrics on the same places, base$\to$retrieval pairs.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Core: layer probes (base vs.\ retrieval), retrieval metrics with baselines, border-crossing and coordinate-perturbation tests, V1--V3 with case studies. Optional: H3 adjacency, SoundingEarth audio with LLM re-captioning, multimodal comparison.
\item \textbf{Subspace amplification} is dropped. Coordinate perturbation stays; it shows whether rankings respond to coordinate \emph{text}, not \emph{why}.
\item \textbf{Metrics and framing.} Per (origin, genre) query over the genre-eligible pool: Spearman $\rho$ between cosine and Haversine ranks (does the \emph{whole} ranking follow distance?), overlap@10 with the 10 nearest (is the \emph{top} a user sees right?), and excess mean km of the top 10 (\emph{how far off} are the misses?). High $\rho$ with low overlap means coarse order but wrong neighbors. Baselines: random ordering of the pool (1,000 permutations) and the Haversine sort. Framing: \emph{which notion of proximity embeddings encode} (same-country and same-language as alternatives). \textbf{Decodable vs.\ reflected:} probe results are reported as \emph{decodable}, retrieval metrics as \emph{reflected in retrieval}. On the same stations, over blocks with $\ge$30 stations and $\ge$3 query origins, we report the Spearman correlation between final-layer probe gain and mean excess km. High gain with large excess is \emph{consistent with} geography that is decodable but not reflected in retrieval; this is correlational, not proof the model ignores it.
\item \textbf{Small fixes.} New title; model count clarified (Jina dropped; EmbeddingGemma has no released base); typos fixed; SoundingEarth uses recorded coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
